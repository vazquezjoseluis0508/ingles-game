# Grammar — Impersonal Passive & Reporting Verbs

## 1. Impersonal passive / reporting structures
Para noticias o cuando no queremos decir quién afirma algo.

### It is + past participle + that…
- It is said that he is dangerous.
- It is believed that he is a local man.
- It is thought that the mayor will resign.

Verbos: say, believe, think, report, know, expect, understand.

### Subject + passive verb + to-infinitive
- The man is said to be in his forties.
- The burglar is believed to be a local man.

### Acción anterior: to have + past participle
- He is believed to have left the country.
- The robbers are thought to have entered through a window.

> Hace la frase más **impersonal**: no asumís la responsabilidad de la afirmación.

## 2. Reporting verbs

### Group 1 — verb + to infinitive
agree / offer / promise / refuse / threaten **to**
- Adrian offered to make dinner. · Pablo promised to tidy his room.
- The children refused to go to bed. · Sam's neighbour threatened to call the police.

### Group 2 — verb + person + to infinitive
advise / persuade / remind / convince / tell / encourage / invite **someone to**
- Bill advised me to keep my money in the bank. · Molly reminded Jack to phone the electrician.
- Peter invited me to have dinner. · The boss persuaded Megan not to leave.

### Group 3 — verb + -ing
suggest / deny / admit / recommend / regret / insist on / apologize for / accuse someone of **doing**
- Ryan suggested going for a walk. · Ruby denied eating the chocolates.
- Roger apologized for forgetting my birthday. · The teacher accused Simon of copying Anna's exam.

> También: **admit + that clause** → *He admitted that he had stolen the watch.*
